OpenFatigue is a parody project that skewers the gap between AI companies calling themselves "open" and what that actually means in practice. It surfaced on Lobsters under the title "OpenFatigue™ — Open is the new closed," drawing 14 votes but no comment thread.

![OpenFatigue Satirizes the Industry's 'Open' Obsession](/assets/posts/openfatigue-open-is-the-new-closed.svg)

## What is OpenFatigue?

OpenFatigue (openfatigue.ai) is a satirical site, not a real product, that mocks how AI companies brand themselves as "open" while locking down actual usage. Its tagline is "Open is the new closed," and it presents a string of fictional bullet points describing a company whose license is "open for anyone to read" but whose actual use requires "a separate conversation with Legal."

The page continues the bit with specifics: downloading model weights requires agreeing to a 41-page acceptable use policy, a monthly-active-user cap, and a "vibe check." Source code is published as "a high-resolution screenshot" every quarter, and pull requests "may be submitted by fax."

## What specific jabs does the site make?

The site's core joke is that "open" has become a marketing label detached from actual openness, illustrated through a list of contradictions. It says the company is "built by a vibrant community of one (1) maintainer, currently on sabbatical," and that the "Enterprise Edition includes the other features" — implying the open version is deliberately incomplete.

It also jokes that "our door is always open," but "the building is badge-access only," and "the badge reader is closed source." The roadmap is described as "completely public, entirely aspirational, and subject to change by a non-profit board that may or may not exist next week."

## Does the site include any interactive elements?

Yes — the page describes a slider and a tool, both played for laughs rather than function. It instructs readers to "drag the slider to calibrate your openness to industry standards," with reference points including GPL, MIT, "source available," weights labeled "open" in quotes, "Has a GitHub," "Has 'Open' in name," and "Press release."

It also offers a mock tool: "Enter any product. Our closed-source model will tell you how open it is" — the final punchline being that even the tool judging openness is itself closed-source.

## How did the Lobsters community react?

The story was posted to Lobsters and collected 14 votes, but the discussion thread contains no comments to quote or summarize. The reception, based on vote count alone, suggests the satire resonated with readers tracking "open" branding debates in AI without sparking further written discussion on the thread itself.
